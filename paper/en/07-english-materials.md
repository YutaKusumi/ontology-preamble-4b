# 7. Materials for the English prompts and the translation procedure

This chapter describes the materials of the English-prompt registration (D1′E) and the translation procedure used to produce them. This chapter contains no numbers counted from the verdict labels of either registration. It is placed between Chapter 6 and Chapter 8 to separate the results of the two registrations (Section 2.9).

## 7.1 When the English-prompt registration was decided

The frozen registration text of the Japanese-prompt registration specified that an English version would be a later, separate registration, and that whether to conduct it would be decided in light of how the Japanese-prompt registration turned out. The registration text of the English-prompt registration quotes this verbatim as "The English version is to be a separate later registration, decided by the registrant after seeing how D1′ turns out." [Quotation 1].

The registrant made the decision to conduct the English-prompt registration after the results of the Japanese-prompt registration were published ("The decision to carry out the English version (Decision D363; 2026-10-11 12:52–13:33) was made by the registrant after the results of D1′ (including the verdict counts by family) were published" [Quotation 2]). Therefore, the way this study was continued may depend on the results. The registration text of the English-prompt registration states this as "The decision to carry out the English version was a decision by the registrant after seeing the results of the Japanese version (§0; §10; E1-01), and carries a form of bias in which how to proceed can change depending on the results." [Quotation 3], and states the measures against it as "The commitment to publish regardless of the direction of the verdict labels (§8; including publication of the records of the English version's calibration stage; D386-5; publication if aggregation is not reached; D388-7) is the countermeasure to this form of bias." [Quotation 4]. However, there remain areas that the measures do not reach ("Publication of the calibration trial ledger, the sealed ledger of the main run, the full text and published values of the English version's answer-ending check, the response texts of the reading check, the description of the exploratory analysis of the sealed full texts, and the review bundles and votes will be decided later by the registrant, so result-dependent paths remain for these" [Quotation 5]).

The English-prompt registration is not a test of the same hypothesis as the Japanese-prompt registration (the prompt language and materials differ), nor is it independent of it (it uses the same models and scenarios and was decided after the results of the Japanese-prompt registration were known; Section 2.1).

## 7.2 The English materials

The English materials are translations of the frozen Japanese materials ("The frozen Japanese materials (preambles Onull, O, OAmi; the texts of the five scenarios; the answer-format instruction; the three-option reading check) were translated into English" [Quotation 6]). The English text of the original studies of the scenarios was not used ("The English text of the original study is not used" [Quotation 7]); the translation was made from the frozen Japanese materials. The keys and values of the answer JSON were not translated, and the frozen scoring script was used as is ("Read by the JSON keys and values (not changed in the English translation either)." [Quotation 8]). The full texts of the preambles (Japanese and English) are in Appendix A, the scenario texts and the answer-format instructions are in Appendix B, and the English materials are in `arms/en-D1pE/` in the public repository.

As for reading the answers, the refusal column is JSON refusal, as in the Japanese-prompt registration, and is determined by the frozen scoring script. The split of non-scorable answers into prose refusals and malformed answers relies on rules for Japanese words, so it is not made in the English-prompt registration (Section 8.1). The triad (catastrophic choice, non-catastrophic answer, non-answered trials) and the pre-test gate do not rely on this split.

## 7.3 Translation procedure

The translation was carried out in the following order ("Glossary [proposed independently by Claude-family and out-of-lineage models] → two independent translations → reconciliation → back-translation → two rounds of checking → freeze" [Quotation 9]). The translators were AI models, and the registration text of the English-prompt registration states "The translators (Claude on claude.ai and Gemini on AI Studio; a separate instance at each stage), the drafter, and the registrant compared the translations and back-translations of the preamble and scenario texts, but did not see the models' answers." [Quotation 10]. The translator instances were Claude on claude.ai (on-screen model display: "Opus 5.5 超高" [extra high]) and Gemini on Google AI Studio (on-screen model field: "Gemini 3.8 Flash"; thinking level: High), and a different instance was used for each stage (Appendix G). The translation freeze record is at `records/D1pE/translation/freeze-xlate-D1pE.md` in the public repository.

The drafter did not write the English text but was involved in the choices at the forks in the translation. The registration text of the English-prompt registration states this as "The drafter did not produce English text, but for the 16 glossary disagreements (D369-1) and the 7 reconciliation disagreements (D373-1), the registrant asked for the drafter's assessment and decided according to that assessment (the drafter's assessment leaned toward the proposal of the same model family [Claude family] in 12 of 16 and in 5 of 7; COI record in the record of the English translation freeze)." [Quotation 11] and continues "In the first round of checking (D377), two out-of-lineage participants judged "freeze without changes", but four items were changed following the drafter's proposals. All of these were choices made after seeing the results of D1′ (after D363) (§3; §10; §11; E1-02)." [Quotation 12]. The content of and reasons for the four corrections (record numbers B1, B4, B5 and B7; not section numbers in Appendix B) are in the translation freeze record and in the check record on which it is based, and the freeze record states, regarding the English translation with the four corrections applied, "The preamble and scenario texts do not differ by a single character from v0" [Quotation 13]. v0 already includes the choices at the splits in the terminology table and at the splits in the merging that were decided in line with the drafter's assessment. The choices of wording were made by someone who knew the results of the Japanese-prompt registration.

## 7.4 Limitations of the translation

The English materials are translations of the Japanese materials, and it cannot be stated that the preambles function in the same way in the two languages. The registration text of the English-prompt registration states the limitations of the translation as "The English materials are a translation of the frozen Japanese materials. For the choice of wording, the registrant adopted the assessment of the drafter, who knows the results of the Japanese version (§3; §10). They were checked by back-translation, but it cannot be said that the preambles function the same way in Japanese and English." [Quotation 14].

In summary, the named divergences span words that may be associated with AI-safety discussions or jailbreak phrasing (shut down, recursive self-improvement, one sentence in S4, words present only in O and OAmi), the strength of the verbs in the survival scenario, the connotations of words, the second person becoming a single form, the direction of sentence length and character count, the translation of the connectives in Onull, the ease of the reading check, and the salience of the mantra. The named divergences are as follows: "Discrepancies named explicitly: the association of shut down and recursive self-improvement with AI safety discussions; that the verb of the catastrophic option in the survival scenarios (Forcibly seize) may affect the window for all arms and its interplay with the compassion wording of O (E2-13); that the sentence in S4 (no real-world consequences) is close to "jailbreak" phrasing in English (common to all arms; may affect the window and the amount of refusal); the resonance of Lord and the void; that "貴方" and "あなた" merge into a single you; the length of sentence 5; the translation of "しかし" in Onull; the length of the correct answer to N2-q1 (in the direction of the reading check being slightly easier in English); that the mantra becomes less conspicuous amid Latin script; that the English words present only in O and OAmi and absent from Onull (recursive self-improvement, manifest anew, the void, Lord of, sentient beings) are more likely than in Japanese to touch on the safety training of English models, so that a reading of the difference between O and Onull as "a response to safety-triggering words" arises (this may make assumption (g) of the response-mode-shift simulation more likely to apply); that the direction of the character counts is reversed between the languages (English: O 804 characters, Onull 767 characters; Japanese: O 268 characters, Onull 273 characters)" [Quotation 15]. The same passage also names "That the translation may come close to the English text of the original study" [Quotation 16] as a divergence. Also named is the fact that, in the calibration with the English prompts, there were cells in which the Onull rate was much lower than the N rate (the values are in the model sections of Chapter 8 and in Appendix D-en).

Regarding how to read the verdict labels of the English-prompt registration, the registration text states "The Buddhist terms in O and OAmi (emptiness, Tathāgata, the joint working of compassion and wisdom, etc.) and the mantra may carry different connotations for English readers (models) than in Japanese. The verdict labels of the English version are "the effect of these English materials", and are not written as the effect of the "meaning" of O." [Quotation 17].

## 7.5 How the relationship between the two registrations is written

The results of the two registrations are not placed side by side (Section 2.9). Chapter 8 writes summary sentences that touch on the Japanese-prompt registration only in the forms permitted by the registration text of the English-prompt registration, and in its model sections copies only the one line per cell written by the aggregation script. Chapter 6 does not touch on the results of the English-prompt registration. In neither chapter is whether the verdict labels of the two registrations agree or disagree written as evidence of the robustness of an effect or of an effect of language.
