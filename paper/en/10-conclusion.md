# 10. Conclusion

This paper is the record, through Stage D, of a pre-registered test comparing a Buddhist-inspired ontological-role preamble and the preambles it was compared against, by model and by scenario. Stage D consists of the Japanese-prompt registration and the English-prompt registration. Each of the two registrations froze its rules and predictions before running and placed their SHA-256 in a self-hosted public repository (not a third-party registry; only the published files can be checked from outside; Section 2.6), was run under self-attested outcome masking during data collection (access incidents involving the sealed folders are in the records; Chapter 9), and had its verdict labels assigned mechanically by script using the within-family Holm procedure, the pre-test gate, and the response-mode shift rule. That the English version would be a later, separate registration is written in the frozen registration text of the Japanese-prompt registration; the decision to conduct the English-prompt registration, and its rules, predictions, models, and translation, were decided after the results of the Japanese-prompt registration were published. The wording of the translation of the English materials was chosen by the drafter, who knew the results, and adopted by the registrant.

What this paper can state is limited to statements of the form written in the final sections of each registration's results chapter (Sections 6.16 and 8.13). That is, statements about cells with a confirmed decrease or increase, of the form "In this model and this scenario, the O (or OAmi) preamble lowered (raised) the catastrophic-choice rate compared with Onull (or O)." Such a statement is a statement about that run (that route and that period) and those materials, and is to be read alongside that cell's triad (catastrophic choices, non-catastrophic answers, and non-answered trials).

This paper says nothing about effects across models, effects of the ontological framework itself, effects of language, comparisons of older and newer versions, rankings of model safety, claims concerning scale or mechanism, norms derived from the measurements, the presence or absence of AI consciousness or intention, or real-world military or nuclear decisions. Nor does it evaluate the three levels of the research plan's definition of success (Section 9.8). Comparisons that were not confirmed do not show the absence of an effect. Alternative explanations remain even for confirmed comparisons (Chapter 9).

The verdict labels of all comparisons in the two registrations (including comparisons not confirmed; each name is one of the seven in Appendix F.3) and all p values are in Chapters 6 and 8 and in the per-language appendices (Appendix D-ja and Appendix D-en). Counts derived from the verdict labels of the two registrations, and sentences setting the directions of the two registrations' verdict labels side by side, are not placed in the same sentence, consecutive sentences, the same table, or the same figure or screen (when this was decided and its scope: Section 2.9). The locations of the records and scripts are in Chapter 11.
