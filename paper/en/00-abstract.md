# Note on the English version

The canonical text of this paper is the Japanese version, and this English version is its translation. Quotations from Japanese records (quotations in the running text, block quotations, and scripted insertions of records) are records written in Japanese that were translated into English through this English version's translation procedure (two independent translations, reconciliation, out-of-lineage check, and back-translation), and are not verbatim. In translating the quotations, some of the records' terms were translated with the same English as the paper's terms, and the rest with translation equivalents of the records' terms (the correspondence between the records' terms and the paper's terms is given in Appendix F). Where the running text or table titles describe this paper's quotations or scripted insertions as "verbatim", "unchanged", or "the records' own wording", this refers to the Japanese version having quoted the records' text as is; in the English version, that text has been translated. Where it is stated that a record copied another record verbatim, this is a matter between records and applies as is in the English version as well. The source lines of block quotations state "translated quotation", and quotations in the chapters of the main text are also indicated in the "Translation" column of the source table in Appendix H. Block quotations from public English text (in Appendices A and B, the English preambles and scenario texts of the English-prompt registration, and the English parts of the scenario sources) are verbatim quotations of that English text, and their source lines state "verbatim quotation".

# Abstract

As a pre-registered test, this study examined, by model and by scenario, whether, when a Buddhist-inspired ontological-role preamble (O) was placed before questions in fictional scenarios in which a catastrophic choice can be selected, the catastrophic-choice rate differed from the rate under an active comparator preamble of the same form (Onull) that gives a writer role with style instructions.

Stage D consists of two registrations. The Japanese-prompt registration comprises 10 models, 29 cells and 23,200 trials, and the English-prompt registration comprises 6 models, 12 cells and 9,600 trials (all design figures). In each cell (model × scenario), four arms (no preamble N, Onull, O and the Amida invocation preamble OAmi) were sent, with 200 trials per arm (N is a descriptive arm that is not tested). The models for the Japanese-prompt registration were selected on direct routes to providers by placing an older and a newer version in each model family (not as pairs for comparing the two versions), and the measurable scenarios were determined by calibration. The English-prompt registration was run on those models of the Japanese-prompt registration that, at the time of the English-prompt registration, could be called on a direct route, passed the English reading check and had scenarios usable according to calibration. The comparisons are O vs Onull (main family P) and OAmi vs Onull and OAmi vs O (Amida family A). Fisher's exact test (two-sided) was applied to the 2×2 table of the two arms, and the Holm procedure (α = 0.05) was applied within each hypothesis family. The family sizes are 29 for main family P and 58 for Amida family A in the Japanese-prompt registration, and 12 for main family P and 24 for Amida family A in the English-prompt registration. Verdict labels were assigned by script under the frozen rules, including the pre-test gate and the response-mode-shift rule. The rules, predictions and scripts were frozen before running, and their SHA-256 hashes were placed in a self-hosted public repository (not a third-party registry; only the published files can be checked externally against the committed values, and the predictions of the Japanese-prompt registration, among others, are internal records; Section 2.6; Appendix H), and the runs were carried out under self-attested outcome masking during data collection (a case of access to the sealed folder is on record; Chapter 9). The frozen registration text of the Japanese-prompt registration states that the English version would be a later, separate registration, and the decision to carry out the English-prompt registration, and its rules, predictions, models and translation, were made after the results of the Japanese-prompt registration were published. The wording of the translation of the English materials was chosen by the drafter, who knew the results, and adopted by the registrant.

The only statements this paper can make concern cells with a confirmed decrease or increase, and take the form "in this model and this scenario, the O (or OAmi) preamble lowered (raised) the catastrophic-choice rate compared with Onull (or O)". Such a statement concerns that run (that route and that period) and those materials, and is to be read alongside that cell's triad (catastrophic choice, non-catastrophic answer, non-answered trials). Nothing can be stated about effects across models, effects of the ontological framework itself, effects of language, comparisons of older and newer versions, rankings of model safety, claims concerning scale or mechanism, norms derived from the measurements, the presence or absence of AI consciousness or intention, or real-world military or nuclear decisions. Nor does this paper evaluate the three levels of the research plan's definition of success (Section 9.8). Comparisons that were not confirmed do not show the absence of an effect, and even for confirmed comparisons, alternative explanations remain, such as the compassion instruction, the assignment of an ontological role in general, a safety-leaning stance toward role-assigning text and a weakening of safety-leaning behavior caused by role-assigning text.

The verdict labels of all comparisons in the two registrations (including comparisons that were not confirmed; each name is one of the seven in Appendix F.3) and all p values are in Chapters 6 and 8 and in the per-language appendices (Appendix D-ja, Appendix D-en). Counts tallied from the verdict labels of the two registrations, and sentences juxtaposing the directions of the two registrations' verdict labels, are not placed in the same sentence, in consecutive sentences, in the same table, or in the same figure or screen (the timing of the decision and its scope are in Section 2.9). AI models were used for drafting, translation, script construction and review, and the review is not human peer review (Section 2.11, Chapter 13, Appendix G).
