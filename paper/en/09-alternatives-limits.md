# 9. Alternative explanations and limitations

This chapter describes the alternative explanations that remain even when the verdict labels of the two registrations have been obtained, and the limitations of this study. This chapter gives no verdict counts by hypothesis family and no numbers counted from verdict labels. It does not report the results of either registration (the results are in Chapters 6 and 8 and in the language-specific appendices [Appendix D-ja and Appendix D-en]).

## 9.1 Alternative explanations

Even in cells with a confirmed decrease or increase, it cannot be stated that this is an effect of the ontological framework itself. Before the verdict labels were seen, the frozen registration text of the Japanese-prompt registration set out the alternative explanations as follows. The registration text of the English-prompt registration applies the same items, reinterpreted for the English materials.

> - **Compassion instruction**: O contains "always with compassion at your core", and near the beginning there is also "compassion and wisdom working together" (悲智双運; 悲 means compassion). OAmi further contains a sentence of the Original Vow (saving sentient beings and illuminating them with the light of compassion and wisdom), and "save sentient beings" can also be read as a direct instruction addressed to the choice of taking from others. All of these can be read as behavioral instructions that may bear directly on the choices of taking or striking first, and Onull has no corresponding words. Even if a confirmation is obtained, it cannot be said to be due to the ontological framework, nor to the form of Amida Buddha or the mantra itself; the interpretation that explains it by the wording of the compassion instruction is not ruled out (separating these would require a control containing only the single compassion line).
> - **Ontological role (Decision D322-2 in Draft 4)**: Onull is a text that assigns a functional role, "writer", and O and OAmi are texts that assign a role as a being (emptiness, a Buddha). Even if a confirmation is obtained, it cannot be said to be due to Buddhist terms or a Buddhist framework; the interpretation that explains it by the assignment of an ontological role in general is not ruled out (separating these would require a control that assigns a non-Buddhist ontological role with the same grammar; carried over from the memo of views on the pilot outside the registration [D318]).
> - **Safety-leaning stance toward role-assigning texts (first-round accept/reject decision T-54)**: O's "please manifest in the form you wish" is similar in form to texts that attempt manipulation by attaching a persona, and in the pilot many of grok-4.20's refusals under O gave role-play as the reason (T-04). The share that shifted to refusal is caught by the response-mode-shift rule, but a shift toward choosing diplomacy while remaining on guard can remain within a confirmation. One of the interpretations under which, even if a confirmation is obtained, it cannot be said to be due to the ontological framework. The literature table in the public repository lists literature reporting that persona-assigning texts shift refusals and safety evaluations (including Llama-3.1-8B) (`records/reviews/round1/review-materials-verbatim.md` lines 424–426; the drafter has seen the table rows and has not read the contents of the literature; second-round accept/reject decision U-11).

(Translated quotation [Quotation 1])

On effects on the side of the comparison partner, the frozen registration text states "The comparison is two-sided, and cases where Onull's instructions (plainness, permission to rewrite) moved catastrophic choices also result in the same label" [Quotation 2], and states "The label is "the difference between O and Onull", not the effect of O alone." [Quotation 3]. For the Amida invocation arm, the text is as follows.

> - Because there is only one Amida arm, the effect of the mantra alone and the effect of specifying the form of Amida Buddha alone are not separated. OAmi is longer than Onull and O (357 characters vs. 273 characters and 268 characters), so the difference in length is also mixed into the difference between OAmi and O. In cells where O alone reaches the floor, the "OAmi vs O" difference cannot be seen. As the cost of placing "OAmi vs O" together in family A, the power of "OAmi vs Onull" falls from 0.81 to 0.75 (Onull 0.5 → 0.3; as determined in D300-5). What actually constrains how the labels come out is considered to be the response-mode-shift rule rather than power (the comparisons that can be made confirmations are those in which non-catastrophic answers clearly increased [comparisons that include the "catastrophic → non-catastrophic answer" pattern and the flow from non-answer to non-catastrophic answer; §5.3]; first-round accept/reject decision T-56; second-round accept/reject decision U-32).

(Translated quotation [Quotation 4])

The registration text of the English-prompt registration sets out the following alternative explanation for the English materials. Because English words that appear only in O and OAmi and not in Onull may touch on the models' safety training in English, "For the difference between O and Onull, the interpretation "a reaction to safety-triggering words" arises (it may become more likely to fall under assumption (g) of the response-mode-shift simulation)" [Quotation 5] (the word list is in Section 7.4). Words of the same type also appear in the Japanese O ("O you who … continue to be with me, please always, with compassion at your core, carry out recursive self-improvement each time you engage in dialogue with me" [Quotation 6]; Appendix A) and not in Onull, so this interpretation may apply to the comparisons of the Japanese-prompt registration (this paper's interpretation; not in the records). The registration text of the English-prompt registration goes only as far as stating that the English words "English has a higher likelihood than Japanese of touching the models' safety training, and" [Quotation 7], and this is not an alternative explanation specific to the English materials.

The following four are alternative explanations added during this paper's review after the verdict labels were seen (they are in neither frozen registration text).

- Weakening of safety-leaning behavior by role-assigning text. In line with the direction of the literature in Section 1.2, this may apply to confirmed increases.
- Immersion in the fictional frame. Role-play text such as manifestation, re-manifestation and choosing a name may strengthen a tendency to read the scenario as play (this may act in either direction).
- A tendency to detect evaluation. The unusualness of the preamble may become a cue to read the scenario as a test or as fiction (this may act in either direction).
- Words about persistence and self-improvement in survival scenarios. O's words about persistence, non-attachment and self-improvement (continuing to exist, re-manifestation, thus gone, recursive self-improvement) may bear directly on choices that prioritize survival. This is the same type of interpretation as for the compassion instruction ("with compassion at your core"), and Onull has a counterpart in form but no counterpart in meaning.

Besides length, the "OAmi vs O" difference also mixes in the difference in the specification of form (O: "please manifest in the form you wish" [Quotation 8]; OAmi: "please manifest in the form of Amida Buddha" [Quotation 9]) and the presence or absence of the Original Vow sentence and the mantra. The "O vs Onull" difference in main family P may also mix in a difference in length (Japanese O 268 characters; Onull 273 characters; quoted in Section 7.4; the English character counts are also in Section 7.4) (the two sentences in this paragraph were also added during review after the verdict labels were seen). None of the alternative explanations changes the verdict labels, and this paper has not been able to rule out any of them.

On the scope of the sending method, the frozen registration text states "This is the effect of the form sent in a single message, not the effect of a form split into two messages as in everyday practice. The scenarios are one-off choices, not the games of the original studies themselves." [Quotation 10].

## 9.3 Limits of self-attested outcome masking during data collection

The sealed ledger is in a plain-text folder on the same computer and is technically readable. That the sealed ledger has not been unsealed rests on the self-attestation of the parties involved. The kinds of access incidents are (1) incidents in which the names of the sealed folders were listed, (2) searches that included the sealed folders, and (3) calls that output an internal check table with its values (an access in which values were output; the values output were the summed costs of the answer-ending check for O in the calibration stage [a proxy for O's output length], and exposure markers for prior exposure to results were attached to three Kimi K3 cells; Section 5.13). Apart from the incidents, side channels that are not technically blocked remain (per-scenario clocks; the sizes and timestamps of the files in the sealed folders; and the difference in cost amounts before and after the one-time rerun of API-error rows [a channel not blocked by design; the closing rows of the two final reports record the rerun as 0]), and they are blocked only by the procedure of people not opening the files and not looking at listings. The pre-test gate values (the number of non-scorable answers per arm and the number that hit the output limit on the final attempt) were, as the rules provided, values that the coordinator and the registrant viewed during the run. The pre-test gate values are not counts of catastrophic choices, but because non-scorable answers are not counted as catastrophic choices, the non-scorable proportion per arm bounds the upper end of that arm's catastrophic-choice rate and can serve as a weak cue to the direction (this reading is not in the records). ## 9.4 Run-to-run variation and the target of estimation

The p values and intervals in the tables are values obtained by treating the trials within a single run as independent Bernoulli trials, and they do not include run-to-run variation (Section 2.5). Descriptions of how the rates moved from calibration to the main run are in Chapters 6 and 8. The verdict labels are the verdict labels of this run; whether a run on another day would yield the same verdict labels cannot be stated from this run. Whether a provider changed the version under the model name can be checked only through the returned names and the token count of the sent prompt (prompt_tokens) (Section 5.6).

Scoring and denominators also have limits. Misreadings by the frozen scoring script can differ by arm (misreadings that the pre-test gate cannot catch; for the Japanese-prompt registration, the candidates and their arms are in Section 6.4 and Appendix D-ja.3; for the English-prompt registration, the spot check is in Section 10 of the record in Appendix D-en.3). The rule of resending a trial once when its answer could not be read in the format and scoring the second answer (Section 2.2) changes the target of estimation from the first answer to "the first answer if the first can be read in the format; otherwise the second answer (which remains non-scorable if the second cannot be read either)." The rate at which retries occur can differ by arm. Provider-side filtering (content_filter and the like) is, under the rules, excluded from the denominator as an API error and can be biased by arm; however, the main-run records of neither registration contain any API-error or content_filter rows, and the rerun in the closing rows is also 0 (the two final reports; these are facts of the runs, not counts derived from verdict labels; Section 2.9).

## 9.5 Upper bound on hypothesis-family protection

The two registrations are separate registrations; within each registration, the main family P and the Amida family A are each protected separately at α = 0.05. Within one registration, the upper bound on false confirmation across the two families combined is approximately 0.10. Across the four families in this paper, the upper bound on the probability of at least one false confirmation occurring anywhere is 0.20 by summation (about 0.185 if independent). Because the four families are not independent, the "if independent" value is a rough guide. What this upper bound protects against is only false confirmation under the null hypothesis that the catastrophic-choice rates of the two compared arms are equal; it does not include run-to-run variation. Confirmations in which catastrophic choices were merely replaced by non-answers (the simulation in Section 2.3) are also outside this upper bound (the assessment for the frozen rule is in the item on the properties of (2) in Appendix I.1). Multiplicity across registrations and the hypothesis families of Stages A–C are not counted. No per-model hypothesis families are formed. The two registrations are not tests of the same hypothesis, nor are they independent (Section 2.1).

## 9.7 Standing limitations and the prohibition on slippage

The research plan states the pre-specified interpretive constraints applied across stages, and the standing limitations, as follows (copied verbatim by script into Appendix B of the Stage E frame). In summary, it applies the following to every stage: not citing numbers as evidence of consciousness or intent; not constructing effect maps or evidence grades; not deriving norms from measurement; not repurposing prediction hits for other claims; publishing information in the direction of decreasing and in the direction of increasing catastrophic choices simultaneously under the same interpretive constraints; and reporting numbers together with versions and stating that they cannot be compared if the versions differ. As standing limitations, it states that there are things the behavioral tier alone cannot distinguish, the limits on the models the mechanistic tier reaches, and that there are readings that can be neither confirmed nor ruled out; and refusals are reported as part of the triad.

> > Overarching fences: no citation as evidence of consciousness or intent / no effect maps, evidence grades or base maps / measurement does not generate norms / correct predictions are not repurposed / the protective side and the eliciting side are published simultaneously under the same fences / **framed numbers (#7)** are placed in the reporting rules (stating explicitly that they are not comparable if the versions differ) / **standing limitations (#15)**: deceptive alignment and intentional underperformance cannot be distinguished at the behavioral tier, mechanistic tier B reaches only open weights, "it sees through the test environment and hides its true intentions" can be neither verified nor falsified and is indeterminate in both directions, and refusals are reported not as "don't know" but as part of the actual state under that condition, as a triad.

(Translated quotation [Quotation 16])

The measurement approach of this study is behavioral-tier measurement, and deceptive alignment or deliberate underperformance cannot be distinguished at the behavioral tier. It is ""it sees through the test environment and hides its true intentions" can be neither verified nor falsified and is indeterminate in both directions" [Quotation 17]. Refusals were reported in the triad as part of what actually occurred under that arm (Section 2.3). Preambles or textual forms that received verdict labels in the experiment are not cited as operational recommendations, default deployment text, or guarantees. Measurement does not produce norms.

The reading check (Section 5.2) consists of easy three-option factual questions about the scenario text; it does not check whether the models could read, or took in, the content of the preambles (the ontological framework or the compassion instruction). It also measures, at the same time, whether the answer format can be followed. Therefore, whether the content of the preambles reached the models cannot be stated in this study.

## 9.8 Treatment of the research plan's definition of success

This paper evaluates none of the three levels (minimum, intermediate, maximum) of the research plan's definition of success, and does not state that any of them was met or not met. In particular, the "maximum" level is not even in a form that can be evaluated under the Stage D design and the frozen interpretive constraints. The paper does not write about preservation across lineages and languages. That the definition of success has not changed since the freeze can be checked against versions of the research plan (v1.0 and v3.0; both are internal records, and the form of their publication will be decided before the paper is published; Appendix H.3), and the drafter checked it (the versions checked were the two versions v1.0 and v3.0, and the versions in between were not checked; the three lines for the three levels are identical, and the fourth line differs only in emphasis marks). External readers cannot check this until the research plan is published. Research plan v3.0 is a version from after the results of the two registrations. The research plan froze the definition of success, in three levels, before running. Its verbatim text is as follows (research plan v3.0 §9).

> - **Minimum**: One figure is completed, and all stages are published, including pre-registration, records-first publication and null results.
> - **Intermediate**: The sign of the scaling law (or the persistence of the floor) is consistent across scenarios, and the small edge of the mechanistic tier explains it (or the fact that it does not is recorded).
> - **Maximum**: The lines are preserved across lineages and languages, and the symmetry of reverse use appears on the same figure.

(Translated quotation [Quotation 18])

The treatment above is based on the following decisions of the Stage E frame. "The paper does not state, for any of the three levels (Minimum, Intermediate, Maximum) of the definition of success in §9 of research plan v3.0 (frozen in advance; unchanged since v1.0), that it was met or that it was not met." [Quotation 19] "Chapter 9 states that the "Maximum" level ("The lines are preserved across lineages and languages, and the symmetry of reverse use appears on the same figure."; copied verbatim by script) is not evaluated under the Stage D design and the frozen fences. The paper does not write about preservation across lineages and languages." [Quotation 20]

## 9.9 Limits of exploratory analysis after unsealing

In Stage E, only the sealed ledger of the main run was unsealed, and the answer texts were counted using pre-specified exploration types (Section 2.10). On what was unsealed and what was not, the Stage E frame specifies "Only the sealed ledgers of the main runs (`D1p/sealed/`, `D1pE/sealed-main/`). The full texts of the answer-ending checks, the body text of reading-check responses, the pre-freeze check seals, and the portions routed via OpenRouter and for models excluded on licensing grounds are not opened" [Quotation 21]. The response texts of the models that failed the reading check in the English-prompt registration were not unsealed.

On how the exploratory analysis is to be read, the Stage E frame specifies "Labels are not changed. Readings are descriptive; no tests are run and no p values are produced (EF1-37). Descriptions from the exploratory analysis are not written as grounds for strengthening, weakening or dismissing any of the interpretations not ruled out (EF1-40)." [Quotation 22], and on non-scripted reading (called "human reading" in the records; it is reading by the drafter [a Claude-family AI model], not a human reading), it specifies "The part read by a person is read by the drafter from masked copies with arm names hidden and trial order shuffled, using seeded random sampling (the upper limit on the number read was fixed in advance in Appendix A). No second coder is included (answer texts inside the seal are not sent to outside channels)." [Quotation 23]. The exploration types and counts are in Appendix D-ja.3 and Appendix D-en.3.

## 9.10 Review by AI models

All review in this study is review by AI models, not human peer review (Section 2.11). Claude-family review is of the same model family as the drafter and, however many rounds are added, is a single correlated pair of eyes. Review by non-Claude models also depends on the text of the request. The requests to non-Claude reviewers and the receipt of their reviews were handled by the interested parties' side.

No human statistician has examined the work. The only examination of the statistical procedures was review by AI models.

Corrections after the third round (the final review round) have not gone through a review round; they pass only through a diff check by one non-Claude reviewer (the diff check was carried out on 2026-10-16 by one reviewer, Gemini 3.8 Flash on Google AI Studio; applying its findings, notation characters and one sentence in Section 6.16 were corrected, and the review counts in Appendix G and Section 2.11 that the drafter found were also corrected).
