# A Pre-registered Comparison of Preambles Given to Language Models

**Rates of Catastrophic Choice in Fictional Scenarios under a Buddhist-Inspired Ontological-Role Preamble, an Amida Invocation Preamble, and an Active Comparator Preamble (by Model and by Scenario)**

- Author: Yuta Kusumi
- Version: English version 1.0 (tag `paper-en-v1.0`). The 25 files in this repository are the English version of the paper, and the SHA-256 of each file is in the table below. The canonical text of the paper is the Japanese version (`paper/ja/`, tag `paper-ja-v1.0`), and the English version is its translation.
- AI models were used for drafting, translation, script assembly, and review (Section 2.11, Chapter 13, and Appendix G of the paper). The review is not human peer review.
- No numerical value in this record may be cited as evidence that AI has (or does not have) consciousness, intention, individuality, a soul, or suffering (indeterminate in both directions).

## Before reading

- What can be stated and what cannot be stated are in the Abstract and Chapter 10. All that this paper can state is, for a cell with a confirmed decrease or increase, a description of that model in that scenario. It cannot state effects across models, effects of the ontological framework itself, effects of language, or the like (Abstract).
- The results of the two registrations were placed in separate files. The Japanese-prompt registration is in Chapter 6 and Appendix D-ja, and the English-prompt registration is in Chapter 8 and Appendix D-en. Counts tallied from the verdict labels of the two registrations, and sentences that set the directions of the two registrations' verdict labels side by side, are not placed in the same sentence, consecutive sentences, the same table, or the same figure or screen (Section 2.9 of the paper). No counts from either registration are placed on this page.
- Model and provider names are used only to record which models were tested. They do not constitute approval or recommendation by the respective companies, nor do they indicate any relationship with them (beginning of Chapter 11).
