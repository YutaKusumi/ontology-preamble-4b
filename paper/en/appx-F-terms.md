# Appendix F Table of Term Correspondences

This is the correspondence between the idiosyncratic terms used in the records (the frozen registration texts, final reports, and aggregation reports in the public repository; the records of the earlier stages; and the records of Stage E) and the terms of this paper. Record terms inside verbatim quotations are read with this table.

How to read the symbols: In the running text of the English version, square brackets ([ ]) are used for brackets within brackets, for quotation numbers in the running text ([Quotation n]), for the delimiters of scripted insertions ([Start of inserted record] [End of inserted record] [Start of inserted table] [End of inserted table]), and for English attached to Japanese words in the records (e.g., "〔下見〕" [pilot]). Quotations from Japanese records and scripted insertions of records were translated into English through this English version's translation procedure and are not verbatim (note at the head of the English version). Brackets within quotations and scripted insertions of records are either translations of the records' brackets (such as 〔〕) or English attached to Japanese words in the records (names of record markers such as 〔長さの露出〕 [exposure to output length] were also rendered in English and written in square brackets). Apart from the translation, the only thing the paper added within quotations and scripted insertions is the ※ mark. The ※ is a mark that the build script of the Japanese version attached to the alternative wordings prohibited by the decision not to present the results of the two registrations side by side (Section 2.9) and to the words "reproduction" and "replication"; in the English version, it is placed immediately after the translations of those words. It is not attached in lines that define the prohibited words (if a ※ is already present in the quoted text, the build script stops). The running text of the paper does not use these words for the relationship between the two registrations, or for the relationship between a reader's rerun and the published verdict labels (rebuilding the same values from the published counts is written as "computational reproduction"). The mark was changed from 〔†〕 to ※ because † overlapped with the marker of prior exposure to results. † (the marker of prior exposure to results) and ‡ (the low-power marker) are markers in the tables and figures of Chapters 6 and 8. The source tags 〈public〉 and 〈internal〉 are attached to source lines and source lists, and not to the names of repositories in the running text. Sources for the main-text chapters (Abstract to Chapter 10) are placed in Appendix H.4, and sources for the appendices within each appendix.

## F.3 Names of the verdict labels (terms of this paper)

Confirmed decrease, confirmed increase, not confirmed, withheld (response-mode shift), not assessable (both conditions at floor or ceiling), measurement failure (response-format breakdown), and not run. The explanations in parentheses in the right-hand column of the table in F.1 (such as "significant under Holm, but") are explanations of the meaning, not part of the names. "Measurement failure (response-format breakdown)" also includes arms stopped by a mid-run stop (Section 2.3). As the name of a calibration decision, it is applied to models that had a response-format breakdown in the frozen format and so had no usable scenario, and that had no usable scenario in the alternative format either (the reason there was no usable scenario in the alternative format does not matter; Section 5.2). "Withheld (response-mode shift)" and "measurement failure (response-format breakdown)" refer to different things (the note after F.1).

## Marker

The ※ mark: a mark attached, in quotations and scripted insertions of records, to the translations of the alternative wordings prohibited by the decision not to present the results of the two registrations side by side (Section 2.9) and of the words "reproduction" and "replication"; it is not attached in lines that define the prohibited words (if a ※ is already present in the quoted text, the build script stops). Quotations from Japanese records and scripted insertions of records are translations and are not verbatim (Appendix H). The running text of the paper does not use these words for the relationship between the two registrations (Japanese prompts and English prompts) or for the relationship between readers' reruns and the published verdict labels (recomputing the same values from the published counts is written as "computational reproduction"; Stage E frame §6; EF1-28; Appendix F).
